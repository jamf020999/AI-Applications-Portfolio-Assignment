# Notes from Key Topics Covered

## A07 – AI in Blockchain

### Blockchain Fundamentals
- Blockchain is a decentralized digital ledger.
- Information and transactions are stored across multiple computers instead of being controlled by one organization.
- Blockchain can improve:
  - Security
  - Transparency
  - Data integrity
  - Trust
- Blockchain applications extend beyond Bitcoin and cryptocurrency.
- Other applications include:
  - Healthcare records
  - Supply chain management
  - Education
  - Government services
  - Smart contracts
  - Credential verification

## AI and Blockchain
- AI systems depend on large amounts of reliable data.
- Blockchain can help verify that data is authentic and has not been altered.
- Combining AI and blockchain can improve trust in AI systems.
- Blockchain can provide records showing where information originated and how it has been used.

## AI Data Integrity
- Poor or manipulated data can cause AI systems to produce unreliable decisions.
- Blockchain creates permanent records that can help verify:
  - Data sources
  - Data history
  - Whether information has been modified
- Blockchain can provide an audit trail for AI systems.
- Audit trails can improve accountability and transparency.

## AI and Blockchain in Supply Chains
- Blockchain can track products as they move through a supply chain.
- AI can analyze:
  - Customer demand
  - Inventory
  - Supply chain information
- Combining these technologies can:
  - Reduce waste
  - Improve efficiency
  - Improve product tracking
  - Help identify problems more quickly

### Walmart Example
- Walmart was examined as an example of blockchain and AI in supply chain management.
- Blockchain can track food products throughout the supply chain.
- AI can analyze demand and inventory.
- Product tracking can help identify contaminated food more quickly.

## IPwe Global Patent Registry
- AI and blockchain can also be applied to intellectual property and patent management.
- Blockchain can secure patent ownership records.
- AI can analyze:
  - Patent values
  - Market trends
- This demonstrates applications of blockchain and AI outside finance and cryptocurrency.

## Decentralized AI
- Decentralized AI distributes AI-related data or processing instead of depending entirely on one centralized system.
- Blockchain could support decentralized AI by providing secure and verifiable information.
- Decentralization may be useful when multiple organizations need to collaborate.

## Federated Learning
- Federated learning allows AI models to be trained without sending all sensitive information to a centralized server.
- Organizations can train systems locally and share model updates rather than raw data.
- Potential applications include:
  - Healthcare
  - Banking
  - Other privacy-sensitive industries
- Federated learning can allow organizations to collaborate while protecting sensitive information.

## Ethical Implications

### Privacy
- Blockchain records are designed to be permanent.
- Permanent records can conflict with personal privacy.
- Transparency and security must be balanced against people's ability to control their personal information.
- Possible approaches include:
  - Encryption
  - Permissioned blockchains

### AI Bias
- Blockchain can verify where AI data comes from.
- However, blockchain cannot automatically remove bias contained within the data.
- AI trained on biased information can still produce unfair decisions.

### Transparency
- AI systems can sometimes function as a "black box."
- Users may not understand how an AI system reached a decision.
- Blockchain can create records of AI activity and decisions.
- These records could improve accountability.
- Blockchain itself can still be difficult for average users to understand.

### Automation and Employment
- AI and smart contracts can automate tasks previously performed by humans.
- Automation may affect some existing jobs.
- New technology-related jobs may also be created.
- Workers may need additional training to adapt to technological changes.

## Future Trends

### Decentralized AI Marketplaces
- AI and blockchain may support decentralized marketplaces for AI services and resources.
- These systems could allow participants to interact without depending entirely on centralized organizations.

### Increased Federated Learning
- Federated learning could become increasingly useful for organizations handling sensitive information.
- It allows collaboration without requiring organizations to directly share all their raw data.

### Blockchain for AI Transparency
- Governments and organizations are developing more regulations for AI.
- Blockchain could provide documentation and audit trails for AI systems.
- This could help organizations demonstrate accountability and compliance.

### Edge Computing
- Edge computing was identified as another technology that may become increasingly connected with AI and blockchain.
- Processing information closer to where it is generated can support more distributed systems.

## Technical Challenges

### Scalability
- Some blockchain networks process transactions relatively slowly.
- Scalability needs to improve if blockchain is adopted for larger applications.

### Energy Consumption
- Some blockchain technologies consume significant amounts of energy.
- Proof of Work is particularly associated with high energy consumption.
- Proof of Stake provides a more energy-efficient alternative.

### Usability
- Blockchain systems can be difficult for average users to understand.
- Wider adoption will require:
  - Better education
  - Easier interfaces
  - More user-friendly system design

## Key Takeaways
- Blockchain is much more than cryptocurrency.
- Blockchain can improve security, trust, transparency, and data integrity.
- AI and blockchain can complement each other because AI requires reliable data while blockchain can help verify that data.
- Blockchain audit trails could make AI systems more accountable.
- Supply chains, healthcare, finance, patents, and government services are potential application areas.
- Federated learning provides a way to train AI while reducing the need to centralize sensitive information.
- Blockchain can help identify the origin of biased data, but it cannot eliminate bias by itself.
- Privacy, scalability, energy consumption, and usability remain important challenges.
- The combination of AI and blockchain has significant potential, but technical development must be accompanied by attention to fairness, privacy, security, and accountability.
